Seattle Seahawks Defensive Scouting Report

Executive Summary

This analysis evaluates the Seattle Seahawks defense using NFL play-by-play data. The objective is to identify defensive tendencies, coverage vulnerabilities, and offensive concepts that generate the largest expected point advantage against Seattle. The model evaluates route concepts against defensive coverage types using Expected Points Added (EPA), Success Rate, and sample size.

Key Findings

1. Attack Cover 3 with Vertical Route Concepts

Seattle has shown vulnerability against corner and post routes when operating Cover 3 defensive structures. Corner routes against Cover 3 generated one of the strongest offensive outcomes in the dataset, suggesting opportunities to attack the outside intermediate areas of the field. Recommended concepts:

  • Play-action flood concepts
  • Corner routes
  • Deep crossers
  • Post routes

2. Attack the Middle of the Field Against Cover 2

Against Cover 2 coverage, post routes produced strong EPA results. The weakness of Cover 2 is the space between the two safeties and behind the linebackers. Recommended concepts:

  • Posts
  • Seams
  • Intermediate crossers

3. Avoid Living on Short Timing Routes

Seattle has been effective at limiting lower-risk concepts such as hitches and curls. These plays produce limited explosive value and allow the defense to remain structurally sound. The Patriots should prioritize creating explosive opportunities rather than relying heavily on underneath throws.

Methodology

Data source: NFL play-by-play data. Each play was evaluated using:

  • Expected Points Added (EPA)
  • Success Rate
  • Route Concept
  • Defensive Coverage Type
  • Down and Distance
EPA measures how much a play changes the offense's expected scoring value compared to the situation before the play. Higher EPA indicates a more valuable offensive outcome.
