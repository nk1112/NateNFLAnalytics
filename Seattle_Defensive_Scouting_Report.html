Seattle Seahawks Scouting Report

Executive Summary

This report evaluates the Seattle Seahawks using NFL play-by-play data to identify offensive tendencies, defensive structures, pressure packages, and areas the Patriots can exploit. The analysis combines Expected Points Added (EPA), Success Rate, formation usage, route concepts, defensive coverage data, and pressure metrics to create an opponent scouting profile. The objective is not only to describe what Seattle does, but to identify actionable strategies for how the Patriots can attack them.

Key Defensive Findings

1. Attack Cover 3 with Vertical Route Concepts

Seattle has shown vulnerability against certain intermediate and vertical route concepts when operating Cover 3 defensive structures. Corner routes and post routes have generated strong EPA results, indicating opportunities to attack areas outside the deep safety and intermediate zones. Recommended concepts:

  • Play-action flood concepts
  • Corner routes
  • Deep crossers
  • Post routes

2. Attack the Middle of the Field Against Cover 2

Cover 2 defenses often create space between the safeties and behind the linebackers. Seattle has allowed efficient outcomes against certain middle-field route concepts, creating opportunities for:

  • Posts
  • Seam routes
  • Intermediate crossers
  • Play-action throws behind linebackers

3. Avoid Predictable Short Passing Concepts

Seattle has been effective against lower-risk routes such as hitches and curls. The Patriots should avoid becoming predictable with underneath throws and should prioritize creating explosive opportunities through formation variation and route combinations.

Seattle Route vs Coverage EPA Matrix

This visualization evaluates Seattle's defensive coverage tendencies and identifies offensive route concepts that generate the most value. The analysis combines route concept, defensive coverage type, EPA generated, and success rate. Higher EPA indicates stronger offensive performance against that coverage structure.

Seattle Offensive Formation Identity

This analysis evaluates Seattle's offensive formations based on frequency and efficiency. The purpose is to identify which formations define Seattle's offensive identity and which packages create the most offensive success. The x-axis represents how frequently Seattle uses each formation. The y-axis represents average EPA generated from that formation. Larger bubbles represent formations with higher success rates. High-volume, high-efficiency formations should receive the most attention during defensive preparation.

Seattle Pass Rush Profile

This analysis evaluates how Seattle generates pressure by measuring the number of defenders sent after the quarterback. The objective is to determine whether Seattle creates defensive success through traditional four-man rushes or through additional pressure packages. Metrics included:

  • Number of pass rushers
  • Number of plays
  • EPA Allowed
  • Pressure Rate
Negative EPA Allowed indicates Seattle is limiting offensive efficiency. Positive EPA Allowed indicates offenses have generated success against that pressure package.

Recommended Patriots Offensive Strategy

Seattle Defensive Look Recommended Patriots Attack
Cover 3 Play-action, corner routes, posts, flood concepts, deep crossers
Cover 2 Attack middle field with posts, seams, and intermediate routes
Man Coverage Motion, slants, crossers, pick concepts, and matchup isolation
Heavy Pressure Packages Screens, quick throws, hot routes, and fast protection adjustments

Patriots Game Plan Summary

  • Identify Seattle coverage before the snap using motion and formation variation.
  • Attack defensive structures that generate positive offensive EPA.
  • Create explosive plays instead of relying only on short completions.
  • Use quick passing concepts when Seattle increases pressure.
  • Force Seattle to defend multiple formations and personnel packages.

Methodology

Data source: NFL play-by-play data. Each play was evaluated using:

  • Expected Points Added (EPA)
  • Success Rate
  • Route Concept
  • Defensive Coverage Type
  • Offensive Formation
  • Pressure Package
  • Down and Distance
EPA measures how much a play changes expected scoring value compared to the situation before the play. Higher EPA represents a more valuable offensive outcome.
